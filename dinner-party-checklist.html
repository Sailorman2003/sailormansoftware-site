Dinner Party Planning Checklist & Timeline

What to do, and when, for a relaxed evening at home

A good dinner party is mostly finished before the doorbell rings. Here is a timeline to work from, whether you are cooking for four or for twelve.

Two to three weeks before

Set the date and the guest list

  • Pick the date and the time, and check them against your own calendar first.
  • Count how many people your table seats comfortably. That number is the ceiling for the guest list.
  • Invite your guests, and ask about dietary restrictions when you do. It is far easier to ask now than to find out at the table.
  • Decide on the style of service: plated courses, family-style platters, or a buffet. It changes how much you cook and how many dishes you wash.

One to two weeks before

Plan the menu and the wine

  • Choose the menu. Make one dish the ambitious one and keep the rest simple — ideally things you have cooked before.
  • Check every course against your guests’ dietary restrictions. One dish everyone can eat is better than a separate plate for one person.
  • Look back at what you served these guests last time, so nobody is given the same dinner twice.
  • Choose the wine to suit the main course. Allow about half a bottle for each guest who drinks, and have something good on hand for those who don’t.
  • Check the equipment: chairs, plates, glasses, serving dishes, and a pan big enough for the main course.
  • Follow up with anyone who hasn’t replied.

The week of

Count, scale and shop

  • Confirm the final number of guests.
  • Scale each recipe to that number, so the quantities are right the first time.
  • Write one shopping list for the whole meal, grouped by the aisles of your store.
  • Buy the wine, the pantry items and anything else that keeps.
  • Write a cooking schedule, working backwards from the time you want to sit down.

The day before

Do everything that can be done early

  • Buy the fresh ingredients.
  • Make whatever keeps: desserts, sauces, soups, dressings. Do the chopping you can.
  • Set the table, and clear space in the refrigerator.
  • Chill the white wine and the sparkling water.
  • Choose the music.

The day of

Cook, then stop

  • Follow the cooking schedule, and aim to be done with the last task an hour before guests arrive.
  • Open the red wine.
  • Empty the dishwasher and the trash, so the cleanup starts from clean.
  • Leave yourself half an hour to get ready. Guests remember a relaxed host longer than a perfect sauce.

Afterwards

Write it down

  • Record what you served and who came.
  • Note which dishes were a hit, and which wine people enjoyed.
  • Note what you would change next time.

This is the step most of us skip, and the one we wish we hadn’t the next time we invite the same friends.
